As a test-set stability diagnostic, individual restarts had lower mean cost than the better online MIQP in 0/5 at 20 EUR/h, 0/25 at 40 EUR/h, 0/5 at 80 EUR/h; this diagnostic did not alter validation-based selection.
At 20 EUR/h, the one-sided upper bound against no storage was -44.6 EUR/day; superiority was established.
The zero-action feasible incumbent was retained in 0/1 batches at 20 EUR/h, 0/5 batches at 40 EUR/h, 0/1 batches at 80 EUR/h; retained fallback outcomes are not attributed to neural control.
Validation selected the threshold-rule baseline as 20 EUR/h: zero action, 40 EUR/h: zero action, 80 EUR/h: zero action; a zero-action choice remains labeled as such in its tuning log.
A complete five-restart batch used 23.5 minutes of recorded training-run time, validation-controller computation, and selected-checkpoint loading. Among trained selections, mean checkpoint loading was 0.02 s. At 96 calls per day, the measured online controller time was 0.30 s/day for the offline policy and 101.27 s/day for conditional-mean MIQP.
On compute time alone, assigning the same unit value to offline and online seconds, the measured offline work equals the accumulated online time saving after 5.3--12.2 deployment days relative to the better of the two online MIQPs. The main paper's break-even condition also includes the observed operating-cost difference for any chosen monetary value of compute time.
