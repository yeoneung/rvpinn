\section{Evaluation protocol and numerical checks}

The benchmark uses IT\_NORD data from 2016--2017 for training, 2018 for
validation, and 2019 for test. All controllers share the economic objective,
15-minute update interval, feasible action set, and terminal treatment.
Checkpoint and restart selection use validation cost alone; batch selection
starts from the feasible zero-action policy. All action-grid candidates lie within the
computed feasible interval before objective evaluation. Evaluation
configurations and input/output hashes are documented in the GitHub repository
(Section~\ref{sec:code-availability}).

Reference-value attribution and nested scenario-count sensitivity with
$M\in\{2,8,16\}$ and three fixed streams are exploratory analyses. They
address the contribution of learning and the sensitivity to comparator
resolution, separately from the primary comparisons. The high-fee
15-second analyses describe sensitivity to the solver budget; the primary
online comparison uses five seconds per solve.

The controller set reported in the main paper is:
\begin{enumerate}
\item no storage;
\item validation-tuned price-threshold control;
\item conditional-mean convex-envelope MPC;
\item conditional-mean exact-band mixed-integer quadratic program (MIQP);
\item reflected two-scenario, two-stage exact-band MIQP with a shared first
action;
\item the no-learning reference-value one-step controller; and
\item the complete offline learned procedure: five restarts, historical
validation selection against a zero-action feasible incumbent, one-step
hard-cost deployment, and the common safety set.
\end{enumerate}
The central fee cell contains five independent five-restart batches. No
storage is a scale reference. Comparator scope is kept explicit: the primary online
controls are the conditional-mean and scenario-based exact-band MIQPs, and the
convex envelope isolates tariff representation. All reported methods use the
common evaluation protocol.

The price rule charges below a lower price threshold, discharges above an
upper threshold, and otherwise holds. Candidate thresholds use training-price
quantiles; the inactive no-action rule is included. The pair is selected by
hard common cost on the same 30 validation days used for checkpoint selection.

\section{Data processing and calibration}

The OPSD 60-minute release dated 2020-10-06 \citep{OPSD2020} supplies local load, day-ahead
price, solar, and wind. Renewable components are summed with at least one
available component. A calendar year is eligible only if all required series
have no more than 1\% missing rows. Gaps of at most two consecutive hours are
interpolated in time; days with a remaining missing value are removed.

The IT\_NORD record has four consecutive eligible years and 35,040 cleaned
hours. Before interpolation, missing fractions are 0 for load,
$2.852\times10^{-4}$ for price, and $2.567\times10^{-4}$ for renewable
generation; all are zero afterward. Conversion to Europe/Rome time precedes
season and weekday classification. Daylight-saving days with other than 24
rows are excluded from daily evaluation.

Only training data determine the load median, renewable 95th percentile,
hourly regime medians, Ornstein--Uhlenbeck (OU) coefficients, correlation, and
reflection box. Raw
load and renewable shapes are scaled to a 500-kW base microgrid. Prices are
converted from EUR/MWh to EUR/kWh. The battery has 1,000 kWh capacity, 500 kW
charge and discharge limits, 0.95 one-way efficiencies, and an SoC interval
$[0.1,0.9]$. Initial and target SoC are 0.5. The linear throughput coefficient
is $300/(2\times4000)=0.0375$ EUR/kWh. The common terminal coefficient is
$\lambda_T=10{,}819.79$ EUR per squared SoC fraction: a 0.10 terminal
deviation costs 25\% of the median complete-day no-storage bill in training.
It is then held fixed across methods. The cycle-based proxy is used for
controlled comparison rather than as an electrochemical lifetime model
\citep{Xu2018}.

For each regime, a zero-intercept first-order autoregressive coefficient is fitted by least squares
to within-day consecutive residual pairs. The exact discrete-time mapping gives
$\kappa=-\log(\phi)$ per hour and maps the innovation variance to $\sigma$.
The cross-process parameter $\rho$ is set to the correlation of standardized
hourly load and price innovations, used as a proxy for instantaneous
correlation rather than a joint continuous-time fit. Reflection bounds equal the
training residual range widened by 20\% on each side.

\section{Exact-band MIQP formulations}

For each horizon index $h$, introduce nonnegative charge $u_h^c$, discharge
$u_h^d$, import $g_h^+$, export $g_h^-$, and binary band variable $z_h$. The
signed action is $a_h=u_h^d-u_h^c$, and
\begin{align}
 s_{h+1}&=s_h+\frac{\Delta}{E_{\max}}
 \left(\eta_cu_h^c-\frac{u_h^d}{\eta_d}\right),\\
 g_h^+-g_h^-&=n_h-u_h^d+u_h^c.
\end{align}
Taper and one-step safe constraints are imposed separately on charge and
discharge. Direction binaries $d_h^c,d_h^g\in\{0,1\}$ impose
\begin{align}
u_h^c&\leq a_c d_h^c,& u_h^d&\leq a_d(1-d_h^c),\\
g_h^+&\leq\overline g_h^+d_h^g,&
g_h^-&\leq\overline g_h^-(1-d_h^g).
\end{align}
They are present at every price: dominance of simultaneous flows at positive
prices is not sufficient for arbitrary time-limited incumbents.
For valid bounds $\underline g_h\leq g_h\leq\overline g_h$, the
band logic uses
\begin{align}
 g_h&\leq G_{\rm thr}+(\overline g_h-G_{\rm thr})z_h,\\
 g_h&\geq G_{\rm thr}+\epsilon
 -(G_{\rm thr}+\epsilon-\underline g_h)(1-z_h).
\end{align}
Here $\epsilon=10^{-5}$ kW and SCIP's feasibility tolerance is $10^{-9}$.
The separation specifies a numerical representation of the strict band
boundary, not a tolerance in the realized bill. Before holding the first
action, native clipping enforces the common safe interval. If the incumbent
has $z_0=0$ but its recovered action exceeds the band by at most $\epsilon$,
the action is moved to a verified fee-inactive crossing only when that
crossing is feasible and the change is at most $\epsilon$. Otherwise no band
recovery is applied. Raw and held actions, direction flows, band status, and
the recovery size are retained in each solve record. Every realized bill uses
$\mathbf{1}\{N-a>G_{\rm thr}\}$ without a tolerance.
The objective is
\begin{equation}
 \sum_h\Delta\left[c_hg_h^+-\alpha_sc_hg_h^-
 +\lambda_1(u_h^c+u_h^d)+c_fz_h\right]
 +\lambda_T(s_H-s_{\rm tar})^2.
\end{equation}
Thus the terminal treatment is identical to that of the learned controller.
An epigraph constraint passes the quadratic terminal term to SCIP
\citep{Achterberg2009}. A hard terminal equality is used only in an ablation.

The stochastic MIQP replicates these variables by scenario. Charge and
discharge at $h=0$ are equal across scenarios. Later variables are
scenario-contingent and the objective is the arithmetic scenario mean. Exact
OU transition variances are used. When the two mean-reversion rates differ,
the innovation covariance over one interval is
\begin{equation}
 \rho\sigma_y\sigma_p
 \frac{1-\exp\{-(\kappa_y+\kappa_p)\Delta\}}
 {\kappa_y+\kappa_p}.
\end{equation}
Each simulated error is reflected into its training box. A no-storage primal
start supplies an incumbent before the online solve.
The scenario-count analysis draws one pool of 16 paths per stream,
date, and decision epoch, then uses its first $M$ paths. The samples are
therefore nested across $M=2,8,16$. Streams 7301, 7302, and 7303 are all
reported under the same five-second budget and one-thread SCIP setting.

\section{Learned value and deployment details}

OpenAI Codex (OpenAI; accessed September 2026) assisted with code review and
experiment and Matplotlib visualization scripting. It was not a controller or
a source of simulated or historical observations. The author reviewed the
scripts and figures, verified references and numerical outputs, and retains
responsibility for the research design and reported results.

The independent decision gate is a development-region winter problem with
state $(S,Y)$, deterministic price error $P=0$, and $c_f=0$. It keeps the
24-hour horizon and battery model but is not evidence about the band-fee
mechanism. Monotone finite-difference policy iteration is run on
$21\times21\times25$ and $31\times31\times37$ SoC--error--time grids; neural
value and action metrics use 5,000 fixed Sobol audit states. Threshold-specific
correctness is tested separately by the hard one-step search, dense-grid
comparison, and realized threshold-occupation diagnostics.

For the selected four-PI gate checkpoint, the fraction of audit actions with
absolute error above 10\% of rated power (50 kW) was 35.2\%.
Mean and 95th-percentile local Hamiltonian regret on the uniform audit design
were 6.14 and 17.70 EUR/h; the maximum was 597.53 EUR/h.
These diagnostics complement the aggregate policy-cost comparison in the
main paper without implying uniform decision accuracy.

The network has five hidden layers of width 96, hyperbolic tangent activation,
and time Fourier features at harmonics 1, 2, and 3. Inputs are affinely mapped
to $[-1,1]$, and all PDE derivatives use float64 automatic differentiation.
The correction network is trained for at most 2,000 Adam steps in each of four
policy-iteration rounds, followed by at most 80 L-BFGS iterations. Training,
adaptive candidates, tuning validation, final audit, and test simulation have
separate fixed seeds. Residual-adaptive refinement is disabled in the
benchmark configuration because the decision gate did not justify its added
complexity.

For a fixed policy, the loss sums the mean squared PDE residual divided by
$\beta^2$ and unit-weight Neumann penalties on the exogenous-state faces.
The latter square $(y_{\max}-y_{\min})\partial_y v/B$ and
$(p_{\max}-p_{\min})\partial_p v/B$ on their respective faces, where
$B=B_0+\lambda_T\max_{s\in[s_{\min},s_{\max}]}(s-s_{\rm tar})^2$ and $B_0$
is the training-period no-storage daily-cost scale. SoC admissibility is
handled by the action set, and the ansatz enforces the terminal value.
Adam uses 2,048 interior and 256 boundary points per step; L-BFGS uses fixed
designs of 8,192 and 2,048 points. Within a policy-evaluation round, synthetic
residual RMS selects the Adam iterate and rejects a worsening L-BFGS update.
Historical economic validation instead selects among completed PI checkpoints
and restarts.

The reference-policy term integrates the no-storage smooth cost on the OU
conditional-mean path by 24-point midpoint quadrature. Its final layer is zero
initialized. Finite differences in physical time, SoC, load error, and price
error check the automatic-differentiation chain rule after adding this term.
The interpolated profiles are piecewise linear, so a time-difference stencil
may straddle a slope change of the quadrature reference.

For the no-learning counterfactual, the neural correction is set identically
to zero before the hard one-step minimization. The no-storage running part of
the next reference value is action-independent; after dropping this common
constant, the code minimizes the exact current hard cost plus
$\lambda_T(s_{k+1}-s_{\rm tar})^2$. Tests compare all pairwise candidate-value
differences and the minimizing action with a zero-correction network. The
counterfactual uses the same 1,025-point structural candidate set and safety
map as learned deployment.

Deployment evaluates the exact hard stage cost plus a learned next value on
1,025 equally spaced feasible actions, augmented by both endpoints, zero, the
tariff crossing, and verified feasible candidates on each nonempty fee side.
An adjacent action value need not change $N-a$, particularly at zero action;
candidate construction therefore also uses adjacent grid-exchange values.
A $3\times3$ Gauss--Hermite rule
integrates the correlated next error. The last decision uses the exact terminal
quadratic. Tests compare the returned decision with a 32,769-point independent
grid and separately verify the tariff crossing.
Because the feasible interval is at most 1,000 kW wide, the uniform-mesh term
in the main paper's hard-threshold grid-defect result is at most 0.977 kW;
the reported denser-grid audit measures the realized objective defect directly.
Reported exact-threshold occupation uses
$\lvert G-G_{\rm thr}\rvert\leq10^{-6}$ kW; fixed 5, 10, and 20 kW
neighborhoods are reported separately.
The post-selection projection ablation instead minimizes the same one-step
objective on $\cA_{\rm cont}(s)$, projects that minimizer to
$\cA_{\rm dep}(s)$, and reevaluates the projected action. It uses the same
selected weights, quadrature, grid size, days, and common cost as direct
deployment and does not feed back into selection.

After batch selection, a finite Bellman audit samples four uniform states at
each of the 96 decision indices for every selected trained central-fee batch.
It recomputes the one-step minimum on a 4,097-point grid with a $7\times7$
quadrature reference and reports residual oscillations and the total
deployed-action defect. A separate 4,097-point search holds the deployed
$3\times3$ quadrature fixed to isolate grid-only defects. The audit also
reports the sampled value change from $3\times3$ to $7\times7$ quadrature and
terminal error. The design and seed are fixed, but sampled maxima are not
treated as continuous-state suprema or uniform quadrature bounds.

\section{Selection, inference, and computation}

Every PI checkpoint is evaluated on the first 30 eligible same-regime
validation weekdays. All checkpoints receive identical days and exact cost
accounting. Sequential selection starts at iteration 0 and changes the
incumbent only for a mean improvement of at least 0.10 EUR/day. One five-seed
batch is then treated as a complete randomized training-and-selection
procedure. For the central winter cell, seeds 0--24 form five consecutive
five-restart batches. At batch selection, zero action is the initial feasible
incumbent and is retained unless a trained winner lowers validation cost by at
least 0.10 EUR/day. Its retention frequency is reported separately; a retained
fallback is not attributed to neural control.

Daily differences are paired by date. The primary interval is a one-sided
95\% circular moving-block bootstrap \citep{PolitisRomano1992} with
seven-eligible-day blocks and 10,000 replicates. At the central fee, each
replicate also resamples the five complete
training batches with replacement; the same sampled date blocks are applied to
learned and comparator outcomes. Outer-anchor inference is conditional on one
complete batch.
For each scenario count, the paired bootstrap includes a third
randomization level: it resamples the three scenario streams with replacement,
the complete learned-policy batches with replacement, and common circular
date blocks. The same resampled dates are used on both sides of each
learned-minus-MIQP difference. Between-stream standard deviations are also
reported without treating three streams as a population estimate.
Two-sided intervals and Holm adjustments \citep{Holm1979} are secondary. No equivalence claim
is based on an interval containing zero, and no noninferiority test is run
without an externally justified margin.

Online time starts immediately before the controller call and ends after the
held action is returned. It includes forecasting, solver or quadrature work,
and action search, but excludes the common post-call safety gate and plant
integration. Daily mean, median, and 95th-percentile latencies are recorded;
tables average the daily summaries rather than pooling calls. Solver tables report incumbent
availability, status, relative gap, node count, and time-limit frequency.
Offline accounting sums each restart's recorded training-run elapsed time,
including post-fit diagnostics and certification, and controller-call time for
all four checkpoints on all 30 validation days. Training manifests have
one-second resolution; their elapsed times replace, rather than supplement,
optimizer-only timers. Selected-checkpoint loading is measured separately
through device synchronization. Process setup before the training manifest,
shared validation simulation and file I/O are outside this accounting. Both
high-fee exact-band MIQPs are also evaluated with a 15-second limit in a
descriptive solver-budget analysis, separate from the primary five-second
deployment comparison.

The representative trajectory date is selected using no-storage output only.
Among the 30 winter dates, the procedure computes the median no-storage
threshold-exceedance duration, chooses the observed duration closest to that
median, and breaks ties by earliest date. All four trajectories---learned,
reference, two-scenario MIQP, and 16-scenario MIQP---use this date; the displayed
learned policy is the first validation-selected batch. Both MIQP trajectories
replay recorded held actions. Learned and reference trajectories use the
reported policies, and all four cost decompositions match their corresponding
daily records. Figure-generation time is not controller latency.

Experiments ran on a Windows 10 workstation with an AMD Ryzen 7 9700X
(8 cores, 16 logical processors) and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4080 SUPER
(16,376 MiB). The environment used
Python 3.9.19, PyTorch 2.4.1 with CUDA 12.4, PySCIPOpt 6.2.1, and SCIP 10.0.
Each mixed-integer solve used one CPU thread. Day-level parallelism ranged
from two workers per scenario stream (at most six concurrent solves) to a
shared pool of at most eight workers. Scenario and seasonal jobs could overlap
with GPU evaluation and short solver diagnostics. Every solve and BLAS backend
remained single-threaded. Execution records document the concurrency settings.
Learned-value training, validation, and held-out evaluation used the GPU;
neural call latency includes transfer of one-step values back to the CPU.
These are hardware-configured controller timings, not a CPU-only neural
comparison or measurements under identical background load.
SCIP numerical-trouble messages are retained in the computational logs and
checked against structured solve-level diagnostics. Controller calls are not
dropped on the basis of solver status or gap availability.
